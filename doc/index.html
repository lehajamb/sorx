Smart order routing · an interactive guide

You decide what to trade.
SORX decides where.

Your strategy decides when and how much to trade. SORX uses the latest available venue prices, displayed liquidity, and your routing limits to choose where to send the order. It then tracks each venue order through fills, cancellations, and recovery.

01 / configured rules, changing market

Fixed rules can produce different routes

Configuration defines which venues SORX may use and the limits it must respect. Market data tells it what is available when it makes a routing decision.

Configured rules

Where may this order go?

Enable venues A and B, allow at most two venues, and set quantity caps and priorities. These rules stay in effect until the configuration changes.

Live inputs

What is available now?

Prices, displayed sizes, market-data health, and order-entry readiness change independently. An enabled venue can still be unavailable for a particular decision.

Routing decision

How should 10 shares be split?

With A offering 4 at 1.00 and B offering 6 at 1.01, allocate 4 + 6. If B later offers 10 at 0.99, a new order can go entirely to B under the same rules.

“Static routes” means configured venue connections. It does not mean every order takes the same path. Updated quotes affect a new decision; they do not automatically move orders already sent to a venue.

What to notice

Displayed depth only. The policy never infers hidden liquidity, and truncated depth remains explicitly unknown.

Order types, depth, and price units

IOC (immediate or cancel): attempt execution now within the price limit; any unfilled quantity is canceled. Passive GTC (good till canceled): the order must be non-marketable across eligible venues, then the whole quantity is placed at one venue.

One child can cover several price levels. Buying 3 shares at 1.00 and 2 at 1.01 on the same venue creates one child for 5 shares with limit 1.01. The limit is the worst allocated price, not a promised fill price.

Examples display prices with two decimal places. Internally, 1.00 is represented by 100 integer ticks. Each venue book has its own version; SORX does not claim a simultaneous cross-venue snapshot.

03 / track the outcome

Sent, filled, or still uncertain?

The original request is the parent order; each order sent to a venue is a child. Venue reports tell SORX how much filled and what remains. If an outcome is uncertain, SORX retains the reservation instead of risking a duplicate trade.

04 / capabilities today

How SORX fits into smart order routing

The Quantt Smart Order Routing Guide describes venue selection using price, liquidity, costs, execution quality, and feedback. Here is where the current SORX implementation fits.

Two implementation tiers. The embedded reference router powers the examples above. The bounded routing components add fixed-capacity processing and supplied execution-quality scores. They are a foundation for integration, not a complete production routing service.

A better quote can change the next route. That is different from learning a better routing policy from historical fills. SORX supports the former; the bounded tier can consume supplied quality metrics, but does not learn them itself.

05 / when something goes wrong

Pause new routing until the state is known

A detected gap in market data makes a venue unavailable for new price-sensitive orders. After a restart, SORX checks saved orders and venue readiness before accepting new routing. An unresolved child keeps routing closed so the same shares cannot be ordered twice.
